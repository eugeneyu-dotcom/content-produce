## A Ritual That Only Works Alone

<p>The elevator game is a summoning ritual that's especially well known across East Asia, Korea in particular, and it comes with more prerequisites than most <a href="/category/bloody-mary-ritual-legend/">ritual games</a> bother with. You need a specific kind of building — at least ten floors, with a working elevator — and you need to be completely alone. Not mostly alone. Alone.</p>

## The Sequence, Exactly as It's Supposed to Go

<p>The order matters, and it's oddly specific: ride the elevator to the 4th floor, then the 2nd, then the 6th, back down to the 2nd, and up to the 10th. Once you reach the 10th floor, you go one more time, to the 5th.</p><p>This is where the instructions get a warning attached to them. At the 5th floor, a woman is supposed to step into the elevator with you. You do not talk to her. You do not look at her. She isn't described as dangerous exactly, just insistently, specifically not something you interact with. The stated reason is blunt: she isn't human.</p>

## The Part That's Supposed to Prove It Worked

<p>After she gets in, you press the button for the 1st floor. If the elevator behaves normally and takes you there, nothing happened; the ritual failed, or you did a step wrong. If instead it ignores the button and climbs back up to the 10th floor, that's the signal. Congratulations, depending on how you want to take that word: you've arrived somewhere that looks like your world but isn't. Same building, same streets outside, different timeline underneath it.</p><p>The rule that gets repeated most often in every version of this I've come across, including the <a href="https://hideandgokill.fandom.com/wiki/Elevator_Game">wiki entries</a> that catalog these games, is that the ritual has to run uninterrupted. No one can talk to you during it, not in person, not by phone. Break that, and whatever you're doing stops counting.</p>

## Why an Elevator, Specifically

<p>Compared to <a href="/blog/hitori-kakurenbo-instructions-safety-guide/">Hitori Kakurenbo</a>, which uses a stuffed doll and a knife as its ritual objects, or <a href="/blog/history-of-spirit-summoning-games/">Kokkuri-san</a>, which uses a handmade board and a coin, the elevator game doesn't need any props at all. It repurposes something that's already unsettling if you think about it for more than a few seconds: a small sealed box that moves you vertically through a building without you doing anything, on the honor system that it'll stop where you tell it to. <a href="https://www.korea.net/Events/Overseas/view?articleId=18358">Korean coverage</a> treats the elevator itself as the actual liminal object, not a doorway you build, but one you're already standing inside dozens of times a week without noticing.</p><table><tr><th></th><th>Ritual object</th><th>What you need</th></tr><tr><td>Elevator Game</td><td>An ordinary elevator</td><td>A tall building, solitude, and exact timing</td></tr><tr><td>Hitori Kakurenbo</td><td>A stuffed doll and a knife</td><td>Specific household objects, a solo apartment</td></tr><tr><td>Kokkuri-san</td><td>A handmade board and a coin</td><td>A group, and a willingness to properly dismiss what shows up</td></tr></table>

## Nobody Has Proof, Which Is Sort of the Point

<p>There's no reliable account of anyone actually completing this and reporting back, which, on its own, tells you almost nothing about whether the ritual works, since the entire structure of the story guarantees an evidence gap. Either nobody's finished it, or the ones who did aren't in a position to post about it on <a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/nosleep/comments/7vfmxb/do_not_play_the_elevator_game_words_of_a_big_time/">r/nosleep</a> afterward.</p><p>What actually stays with me isn't the woman, or the other-world premise, both of which read as fairly standard genre furniture. It's the smaller, quieter question buried in the ritual's own logic: every single time an elevator door opens and you step out, how would you know for certain you'd returned to the same floor, the same building, the same version of everything, that you left? The ritual just makes explicit a doubt that's arguably always been sitting there, underneath something this ordinary.</p>
